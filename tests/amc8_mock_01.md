**Problem 10.**

**Problem 11.**

**Problem 12.**

**Problem 13.**

**Problem 14.**

**Problem 15.**

**Problem 16.**

**Problem 17.**

**Problem 18.**

**1. (E)** Compute step by step: $333{,}333-33{,}333=300{,}000$; $300{,}000-3{,}333=296{,}667$; $296{,}667-333=296{,}334$; $296{,}334-33=296{,}301$; $296{,}301-3=296{,}298$. Units digit: $8$.

**2. (C)** $\frac{55}{11}=5$, $\frac{110}{55}=2$, $\frac{55}{1100}=0.05$. Sum $=5+2+0.05=7.05$.

**3. (B)** Widths add: $2+5+6+9=22$.

**4. (D)** $1+2+\cdots+10=55$. The only perfect square reachable by removing one number from $1$–$10$ is $49=55-6$. She left out $6$.

**5. (B)** Count pairs $(a,b)$ with $4\mid ab$: for $a=1$: $b=4$ (1); $a=2$: $b$ even (3); $a=3$: $b=4$ (1); $a=4$: any (6); $a=5$: $b=4$ (1); $a=6$: $b$ even (3). Total $15$ of $36$ outcomes $=\frac{5}{12}$.

**6. (A)** The conditions give $D$ before $A$ before $B$ before $C$: order $D,A,B,C$.

**7. (C)** Let $T(n)$ be the number of domino tilings of a $2\times n$ board. $T(n)=T(n-1)+T(n-2)$ with $T(1)=1$, $T(2)=2$, giving $1,2,3,5,8$. So $T(5)=8$.

**8. (B)** Track all possibilities: after day 1: $\{2,3\}$; day 2: $\{4,5,6\}$; day 3: from $4$: $6,8$; from $5$: $7,10$; from $6$: $8,12$. Union: $\{6,7,8,10,12\}$ — $5$ amounts.

**9. (A)** Let blue $=x$; red $=2x$; green $=2x+3$. Then $x+2x+(2x+3)=28$, so $5x=25$, $x=5$, and green $=13$.

**10. (D)** $2030-1985=45$ years; $340+45\cdot2=430$ ppm.

**11. (A)** $AB$ is horizontal with length $8-2=6$. Area $=\frac12\cdot6\cdot h=15$ gives $h=5$, so $y=3+5=8$.

**12. (E)** Tanks are $t,\ t+1,\ t+3,\ t+6$; sum $4t+10=66$, so $t=14$ and the 4th tank has $14+6=20$ fish.

**13. (E)** Ways to write $4$ as an ordered sum of $1$s and $2$s: $1{+}1{+}1{+}1$, $2{+}1{+}1$, $1{+}2{+}1$, $1{+}1{+}2$, $2{+}2$ — $5$ ways.

**14. (B)** Compare routes: $A\to B\to C\to D=3+4+2=9$; $A\to C\to D=9+2=11$; $A\to B\to D=3+8=11$. Shortest: $9$.

**15. (A)** $\underline{A}\,\underline{B}-\underline{B}\,\underline{A}=9(A-B)=45$, so $A-B=5$. Pairs $(A,B)$ with $B\ge1$: $(6,1),(7,2),(8,3),(9,4)$ — $4$ numbers.

**16. (C)** A row product is odd only if every cell in the row is odd. Among $1$–$25$ there are $13$ odd numbers; each all-odd row uses $5$, so at most $\lfloor 13/5\rfloor=2$ rows can have odd products. This is achievable, so the answer is $2$.

**17. (D)** There are $\binom{6}{2}=15$ pairs of squares. Attacking pairs: $8$ between adjacent columns (2 horizontal + 2 diagonal per column pair, times 2 column pairs) plus $3$ vertical pairs $=11$. Non-attacking: $15-11=4$.

**18. (B)** Ring area $=\pi(3^2-2^2)=5\pi$; largest circle area $=16\pi$. Fraction $=\frac{5\pi}{16\pi}=\frac{5}{16}$.

**19. (E)** By inclusion–exclusion: $\frac34+\frac23-both=1$, so $both=\frac{9}{12}+\frac{8}{12}-1=\frac{5}{12}$.

**20. (B)** No three vertices of a regular hexagon are collinear, so any 3 vertices form a triangle: $\binom{6}{3}=20$.

**21. (B)** Let yellow $=y$, green $=y+4$; $2y+4=12$ gives $y=4$, so green $=8$.

**22. (D)** Annulus area $=\pi(3^2-1^2)=8\pi$ sq in. Length $=\dfrac{8\pi}{0.01}=800\pi$ inches.

**23. (A)** A segment from corner to corner of an $a\times b$ grid crosses $a+b-\gcd(a,b)$ squares: $3+6-\gcd(3,6)=3+6-3=6$.

**24. (E)** The diagonal of the square equals the diameter $10$, so side $=\frac{10}{\sqrt2}$ and area $=\frac{100}{2}=50$.

**25. (B)** Total seat choices: $\binom{6}{3}=20$; only $\binom{3}{3}=1$ choice puts all three in front. Probability $=\frac{1}{20}$.
